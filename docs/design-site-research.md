# `.design` site research for noahhova.com

Reviewed November 10, 2026. These are applications for Noah's one-page personal site, not copies of another site's branding or artwork. The examples were chosen for relevance; no reliable popularity ranking was established.

| Source | Observed pattern | Useful application here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Microsoft Design](https://microsoft.design/) | Editorial stories are grouped by subjects such as UX Research, Product, Craft, and Design Thinking. | In a future section, index Noah's own math/AI notes with short subject labels and direct links. Keep the painting hero as the introduction. |
| [Octet Design](https://octet.design/) | Project entries explain the challenge and solution, then link to the full work. | In a future section, show two or three real projects with the problem, Noah's role, result, and a live or source link. Do not invent outcomes or client logos. |
| [Hoverstate](https://www.hoverstate.design/) | Its gallery links to live production sites so layouts and motion can be inspected at different widths. | Use live references as a visual QA set: compare Noah's hero, first scroll, menu, and contact section at iPhone and desktop sizes. Keep the current automated viewport tests as measurable guardrails. |
| [Paper](https://paper.design/) | Its design workflow uses real content and web-standard HTML/CSS, with desktop and mobile frames shown together. | Test the actual Noah copy and project links in 320–440px frames before adding content; keep one HTML/CSS experience for phone and desktop instead of a separate app shell. |

When the hero-only scope expands, prioritize real work samples, then dated notes. The hero introduces Noah's interests but gives visitors no work to inspect yet. More motion would make that information gap more visible, not solve it.
